# The HGVSc of an allele whose span runs over a transcript's edge

**The defect in one sentence.** Under `--hgvs`, Ensembl VEP 116.3 reads the whole alternate allele against the reference bases inside the transcript when an allele's span runs over the transcript's edge. A substitution of several bases is then written as a `delins` of every alternate base over the bases inside, and one whose bases inside are all unchanged as an insertion of the outside bases at an intron coordinate before the first exon. A deletion over the edge written with a base it keeps becomes a `del`, a `delins` or a substitution of bases inside. vep-rs describes only the change inside the transcript, and writes no HGVSc where the change lies outside or is a deletion written nowhere wholly inside.

The allele's other end may lie in an exon, in an intron or past the transcript's other edge, and the reading is the same in each case. Where that end lies in an intron or past the other edge, neither end of the allele maps to a cDNA base, and both engines leave `cDNA_position` empty, as they do on an intronic row.

**Why this is a defect.** `21 34915345 TGCTGGGAGCC TGCTGGGCGCC` changes one base, the `A` at 34915352, c.-47 of ENST00000290239, and Ensembl VEP writes `c.-47A>C` for that change given alone, `21 34915352 A C`. Given with its unchanged flanks, which run over the transcript's first base at 34915350, the same change is written `c.-49_-47delinsTGCTGGGC`: three bases of the transcript replaced by eight, a change the record does not carry.

`21 34915340 AGGCATGCTGGGAGCC CGGCATGCTGGGAGCC` changes the `A` at 34915340, ten bases before the first base, and no base of the transcript, yet is written `c.-49-1_-49insCGGCATGCTG`. That coordinate does not exist: `c.-49-1` names a base of an intron before c.-49, the transcript's first base, which no intron precedes. The HGVS numbering continues upstream of a transcript as c.-50. The notation inserts ten bases the record does not insert, and Ensembl VEP itself writes no HGVSc for the same change given alone, `21 34915340 A C`.

Five words name the steps on this page. The clamp is Ensembl VEP's move of each end of the written span onto the transcript, which leaves the reference bases inside it. The clip strips the bases two alleles share at their start and end, and both engines apply it. A cut removes an allele's bases outside the transcript: the clamp cuts the reference, and vep-rs cuts the alternate to match. Trimming reduces a record's alleles to their minimal pair, as `--minimal` does, and the anchor chop drops the first base a VCF insertion or deletion shares with its reference.

**What vep-rs does instead.** vep-rs cuts the alternate allele as the clamp cut the reference and describes the change inside the transcript: `c.-47A>C` for the first record, no HGVSc for the second, since no base of the transcript changes. A substitution is cut position by position. An allele of unequal length has its change aligned 3' within the span first, the shared leading bases before the shared trailing ones, as the trimming of a minimised record aligns it. A change lying inside the transcript is then described there, and one lying outside has no notation, except added bases whose run of placements reaches the transcript's first or last base, which vep-rs places by [the 3' rule](hgvs-added-bases-left-at-the-span-end.md).

A change straddling the edge has the bases inside it deleted or replaced by the change's alternate bases, never by a shared base outside, with one exception: a deletion that can be written nowhere wholly inside the transcript has no HGVSc. `21 34915347 CTGGG CT,CTGGGC` keeps `TGGG` against `T` and `TGGGC` at 34915348-34915351. For `T`, the deletion of `GGG` reaching over the first base, vep-rs writes no HGVSc; Ensembl VEP reads the unchanged `T` against the two bases inside and writes `c.-49_-48delinsT`. The `TG` of `21 34915347 CTGGG CTG,CTGGGC` deletes the two bases inside alone, `c.-49_-48del` on vep-rs, and Ensembl VEP reads the unchanged `T` against the first of them, `c.-49G>T`.

The exception is a deletion written with bases it keeps that runs over the transcript's first or last base. vep-rs aligns the deleted bases 3' within the span, then carries them each way along the chromosome while the base they leave equals the base they take in. Where no placement lies wholly inside the transcript, vep-rs's row has no HGVSc and neither HGVSIntronOffset field: the HGVS nomenclature describes such a deletion on the genome alone (section 7). Neither engine's row has an `HGVS_OFFSET`. The `GGG` above, between a `T` and an `A`, has one placement. A deletion with a placement wholly inside keeps its notation. Under `--shift_hgvs 0` nothing is carried, and the bases inside are described as deleted.

**Where it is in Ensembl VEP's source** (`ensembl-variation` at release/116):

- `Bio/EnsEMBL/Variation/TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 2726-2735, `_var2transcript_slice_coords`: the written span is mapped onto the transcript slice, one wholly outside is refused at line 2729, and lines 2732-2733 clamp each end onto the transcript.
- The same file, lines 1474-1483, `hgvs_transcript`: the clamped span is passed to `hgvs_variant_notation` with the whole alternate allele, and `Bio/EnsEMBL/Variation/Utils/Sequence.pm` line 526 reads the reference allele from the clamped span alone, so the two alleles differ in length and line 616 names a `delins`.
- `TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 2204-2242, `_clip_alleles`: the bases the two alleles share at their start and end are stripped and the span moved with them; lines 2271-2284 re-type an emptied reference as `ins`. A substitution whose bases inside are unchanged keeps its outside bases alone, at a span before the first base.
- The same file, lines 2766-2768 and 2800-2827, `_get_cDNA_position`: the position becomes a chromosome coordinate read against the exons in genomic order. The base before the transcript's genomic start, slice position 0 on a forward strand and the position past the last base on a reverse strand, lies in no exon, and the intron branch places it before the first exon in genomic order. On a forward strand that is the exon's cDNA start with `-1`, `-49-1` after the start-codon shift of lines 2862-2867; on a reverse strand the exon's cDNA end with `+1`, `83+1`, the transcript's last base. The base past the genomic end, the other edge, lies beyond every exon and line 2834 returns nothing.

**What correcting it would change.** Ensembl VEP would write the notation of the change inside the transcript, as it writes for the minimised record, and none for an allele that changes no base of the transcript or deletes over the edge with no placement wholly inside. Release 115.2 wrote none for both: its `_var2transcript_slice_coords` returned nothing for any span with an end outside the transcript (release/115, lines 2656-2661).

**The class it produces.** `hgvs_clipped_reference_against_whole_alternate`: a differing HGVSc on a row of a sequence allele whose cDNA position has an open side (`?-6`, `8485-?`), or is empty where the allele's other end lies in an intron or past the other edge. Ensembl VEP's notation is of the row's transcript, or empty where the whole alternate equals the bases inside; vep-rs's is of the same transcript and numbering, or empty where the change lies outside or is a deletion written nowhere wholly inside the transcript. On a model edited to the genome, Ensembl VEP's notation may also stand at a coordinate slid past the last base, or name the base outside in a substitution's reference (section 8).

Against an empty notation vep-rs's is a `del`, the bases the aligned change deletes inside; against an empty vep-rs notation Ensembl VEP's is an `ins` or a `dup`, what the outside bases read as once the clip has stripped the unchanged bases inside. It may also be a `del`, a `delins` or a substitution of bases inside, what Ensembl VEP writes for a deletion over the edge. The comparator takes one on a row whose cDNA position has an open side and whose allele holds fewer bases than its Location spans; on any other row it stays a difference.

A row whose cDNA position is empty shows no open side, so the comparator reads the clamp from the transcript's exon spans. Given the candidate cache with `--json-cache`, it counts the bases of the allele before the transcript's first base and past its last. Without the cache such a row stays a difference.

With the cache, a substitution's row is the class when both notations are a `delins` and each inserts a run of the row's allele, a stretch of its consecutive bases. The two runs and their coordinates must then account for exactly the counted bases at each end. At the start, the bases by which vep-rs's run begins after Ensembl VEP's, plus the signed distance from vep-rs's start coordinate to Ensembl VEP's, equal the bases counted before the first base. The end is tested the same way against the bases counted past the last base. Each coordinate of either notation must also be the one the exon spans give the allele base its run puts there.

On a row whose cDNA position has an open side, the comparator bounds how far apart the two notations may lie. Both engines clip against the bases inside the transcript, Ensembl VEP the whole alternate and vep-rs the alternate it has cut. So both notations lie within the allele's span over the transcript, Ensembl VEP's one base before it where the outside bases print before exon 1, and an `ins` of either one base after it. Every coordinate of vep-rs's notation therefore lies within the span's length plus one of every coordinate of Ensembl VEP's.

The comparator measures that distance on one scale along the transcript, laid out as the notation numbers it. An intron adds no length to the scale: an intron base stands at its exon base's place moved by its offset. A 3' UTR coordinate stands beyond every coding coordinate, since a notation does not carry the length of the coding sequence. Two coordinates on either side of the stop codon are therefore never within the span's length plus one on that scale, whatever their distance in bases. A row whose cDNA position is empty, as in section 6, is decided by the exon-span test above and is not held to this bound.

The kinds are not constrained: the whole alternate read against the bases inside gives Ensembl VEP a substitution, `delins`, `ins`, `del` or `dup` at any span inside, as the examples show.

The HGVSIntronOffset plugin's two fields follow each engine's own notation, and the class takes them in a few shapes of row alone. [How a comparison treats the HGVSIntronOffset fields of an allele over a transcript's edge](hgvs-clipped-reference-intron-offset-fields.md) states those shapes, how a pair of values is read, and three worked records.

The class is built into the format comparator and the golden harness's classifier and applied once per row. The concordance figures score consequence sets and never see the field. Four records of the committed corpora reach the class, two-base substitutions over the edge of an organelle transcript in the fly, yeast and arabidopsis corpora, and their manifests record vep-rs's notation under it.

A deletion over an edge written with a base it keeps comes from a record of several alternates. The 1000 Genomes Phase 3 dataset on GRCh37 holds 15 such rows. On 14 the deletion can be written nowhere wholly inside the transcript, on 7 of those wholly outside it as well; on the fifteenth a placement lies wholly inside, and vep-rs keeps its notation. Ten of the 14 lie on chromosomes 2, 13, 15 and 17: Ensembl VEP writes a `del` on seven, a `delins` on two and nothing on one, and vep-rs no HGVSc on all ten. Chromosome 6 of the 1000 Genomes high-coverage dataset and four parts of gnomAD v4.1 hold none.

## 1. One changed base inside the first exon of ENST00000290239

**In short.** `21 34915345 TGCTGGGAGCC TGCTGGGCGCC` spans 34915345-34915355 on chromosome 21 of GRCh37, the change an `A` to `C` at 34915352. ENST00000290239, forward strand, starts at 34915350 with a 49-base 5' UTR, so c.-49 is its first base and the record's five leading bases lie before it. Under `--hgvs` both engines write `5_prime_UTR_variant` and `cDNA_position` `?-6`; Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.-49_-47delinsTGCTGGGC`, vep-rs `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.-47A>C`. The change given alone, `21 34915352 A C`, is `c.-47A>C` on both engines, and under `--minimal` both trim the record to that base and agree.

The written span maps to slice coordinates (-4, 6) and is clamped to (1, 6): the reference read there is `GGAGCC`, the alternate passed whole is `TGCTGGGCGCC`, eleven bases against six, a `delins`. The clip strips the shared `GCC` from the end and nothing from the start, since `G` and `T` differ, leaving `GGA` against `TGCTGGGC` over c.-49 to c.-47. vep-rs cuts the alternate's five outside bases first, `GGCGCC` against `GGAGCC`, and the clip leaves the one changed base.

## 2. One changed base before the last base, on either strand

**In short.** `21 34949805 TGGGTTATAAAGTGAA TGGCTTATAAAGTGAA` changes the `G` at 34949808 to `C`; ENST00000290239 ends at 34949812, so the record's last eight bases lie past the transcript. Both engines write `3_prime_UTR_variant` and `cDNA_position` `8485-?`; Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.*1461_*1465delinsCTTATAAAGTGAA`, vep-rs `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.*1461G>C`, the notation both write for `21 34949808 G C`.

`21 9937930 TGTTTGTCCCT TGTTTGTCACT` changes the `C` at 9937938 to `A` inside ENST00000459307, a reverse-strand miRNA transcript of 83 bases whose last base is 9937936. The record's six leading bases lie past it. Both engines write `mature_miRNA_variant` and `cDNA_position` `79-?`; Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000459307.1:n.81_82delinsTGACAAAC`, vep-rs `HGVSc=ENST00000459307.1:n.81G>T`, the notation both write for `21 9937938 C A`. On the reverse strand the alleles are read as their reverse complements and the outside bases trail the span. The clip strips the two shared leading bases and the shared last base, and the alternate's eight remaining bases, five of them from outside the transcript, stand in the `delins`.

## 3. Changed bases on both sides of the first base

**In short.** `21 34915348 TGGG ACAC` changes all four of its bases, two before the first base of ENST00000290239 and two inside, c.-49 and c.-48. Both engines write `5_prime_UTR_variant` and `cDNA_position` `?-2`; Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.-49_-48delinsACAC`, vep-rs `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.-49_-48delinsAC`, the notation both write for the bases inside given alone, `21 34915350 GG AC`. `--minimal` trims nothing here, the alleles sharing no base, so the rows stand under it too.

## 4. Every base inside unchanged

**In short.** `21 34915340 AGGCATGCTGGGAGCC CGGCATGCTGGGAGCC` changes the `A` at 34915340 to `C`, ten bases before the first base of ENST00000290239; the six bases inside the transcript, `GGAGCC`, are unchanged. Both engines write `5_prime_UTR_variant` and `cDNA_position` `?-6`; Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.-49-1_-49insCGGCATGCTG`, vep-rs no HGVSc. The change given alone, `21 34915340 A C`, is `upstream_gene_variant` with no HGVSc on both engines, and under `--minimal` both trim the record to it. `21 34915345 TGCTGGGAGCC CGCTGGGAGCC`, the change five bases before the first base, is written `c.-49-1_-49insCGCTG`: the coordinate is the first base's whatever the change's distance.

The clamped reference `GGAGCC` is stripped whole by the clip, since the alternate ends with the same six bases, leaving an empty reference and the ten outside bases at slice span (1, 0), re-typed `ins`. Position 0 lies before exon 1 and in no exon, so the intron branch of `_get_cDNA_position` places it before the first exon in genomic order, at that exon's cDNA start with `-1`: `1-1`, shifted against the start codon to `-49-1`. vep-rs cuts the alternate to `GGAGCC`, equal to the reference, and writes nothing.

The reverse-strand counterpart is a 54-base record at 9937918-9937971 changing its first base, `G` to `A` at 9937918, eighteen bases past the last base of ENST00000459307 (9937936), the 36 bases inside unchanged. Both engines write `mature_miRNA_variant` and `cDNA_position` `48-?`; Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000459307.1:n.83_83+1insCAAACATTCAGACCCTCT`, vep-rs none; `21 9937918 G A` is `downstream_gene_variant` with no HGVSc on both.

The same change at the genomic-high edge of either transcript, `21 34949805 TGGGTTATAAAGTGAA TGGGTTATAAAGTGAC` and `21 9938010 GCTACGAGGGTCTGAA GCTACGAGGGTCTGAC`, gets no HGVSc from either engine: the stripped span lies past the last exon and line 2834 returns nothing. Each of the two changes its last base alone, at 34949820 and at 9938025, so the first is not the record of section 2, which has its position and length and changes 34949808.

## 5. A multi-allelic record's allele still carrying a shared base

**In short.** Both engines trim a lone insertion or deletion to its minimal pair before annotation, so an allele of unequal length reaches the clamp with a shared outside base only through a multi-allelic record. `21 34915348 TGG TGGA,TGGC` loses its shared first base to the VCF anchor chop and keeps `GG` against `GGA` and `GGC` at 34915349-34915350, over the first base of ENST00000290239. Both engines write `5_prime_UTR_variant` and `cDNA_position` `?-1`; Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000290239.6:c.-49_-48insGA` and `c.-49_-48insGC`, vep-rs `c.-49_-48insA` and `c.-49_-48insC`, the notation both write for `21 34915350 G GA` given alone.

`21 34915348 TGGGA TGGG,TGGGC` keeps `GGGA` against `GGG` and `GGGC` at 34915349-34915352. For `GGG`, a deletion of the `A` at 34915352, Ensembl VEP writes `c.-47A>G`, a substitution, where vep-rs writes `c.-47del`, the notation both write for `21 34915351 GA G`; for `GGGC`, the substitution of that `A`, Ensembl VEP writes `c.-47delinsGC` and vep-rs `c.-47A>C`. Ensembl VEP reads the clamped `GGA` against the whole `GGG` and `GGGC`; vep-rs cuts the shared `G` outside the transcript from each alternate first, since the alternate begins with it.

Where the whole alternate equals the bases inside, Ensembl VEP writes nothing (Utils/Sequence.pm lines 529-531). `21 34915348 TGGG TGG,TGGGA` keeps `GGG` against `GG` and `GGGA` at 34915349-34915351. For `GG`, one `G` deleted from a run across the first base, Ensembl VEP writes no HGVSc and vep-rs `c.-48del`, the last base of the run, where the trimming of a minimised record places the deletion. 

For the `G` of `21 34915348 TGGG TG,TGG`, two bases of that run deleted, the clip of the whole `G` against the clamped `GG` leaves one base. Ensembl VEP writes `c.-48del` where vep-rs writes `c.-49_-48del`, the two deleted bases inside.

An alternate shorter than the bases inside can repeat them: `21 34915346 GCTGG GGG,GA` keeps `CTGG` against `GG` and `A` at 34915347-34915350. `GG` deletes the `CT` before the first base and Ensembl VEP writes `c.-49dup`, the whole `GG` against the clamped `G`, where vep-rs writes no HGVSc; `A` replaces the four bases, a change straddling the edge, and both engines write `c.-49G>A`. `21 34915348 TGGGA TGGA,TGGGAC` keeps `GGGA` against `GGA` and `GGGAC` at 34915349-34915352: `GGA` is the same deletion, no HGVSc against `c.-48del`, and `GGGAC` inserts a `C` after c.-47, `c.-47delinsGAC` on Ensembl VEP against `c.-47_-46insC` on vep-rs.

The 3' alignment also decides an allele over a transcript's last base. `21 27087234 TCC TC,TCCC` keeps `CC` against `C` and `CCC` at 27087235-27087236, the last base of ENST00000312957 and the base after it, both `C`. A `C` deleted from or inserted into that run lies after the last base once aligned 3', the position the trimming gives `21 27087234 TCC TC` alone, a `downstream_gene_variant` with no HGVSc; both engines write `3_prime_UTR_variant`, `cDNA_position` `1693-?` and no HGVSc for either allele.

## 6. An allele from the first intron over the last base, across three exons

**In short.** A substitution of 6,061 bases at 11:116700963-116707023 of GRCh37 starts in the first intron of ENST00000227667, 307 bases after its first exon, and ends 3,235 bases past the transcript's last base, 116703788. The 2,826 positions inside are the rest of that intron, three exons and the two introns between them. Neither end maps to a cDNA base, so `cDNA_position` is empty on both engines. Both write a `delins` from c.-14+307 to c.*188, no base being shared at either end. Ensembl VEP's inserts all 6,061 alternate bases; vep-rs's inserts the 2,826 that lie inside the transcript.

## 7. A deletion over the first base written with a base it keeps

**In short.** `2 213628398 . TAA AAA,T`, a 1000 Genomes Phase 3 record on GRCh37, has an alternate `T` that keeps the reference's first base and deletes the two `A` at 213628399 and 213628400. ENST00000408461, on the reverse strand, runs from 213628294 to 213628399, so 213628399 is its first base, n.1, and 213628400 lies one base before it. Both engines write `non_coding_transcript_exon_variant` and `cDNA_position` `?-2`. Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000408461.1:n.1del`, `HGVS_IntronStartOffset=0` and `HGVS_IntronEndOffset=0`; vep-rs writes no HGVSc and neither field.

The written span maps to slice coordinates (0, 2) and is clamped to (1, 2), the transcript's first two bases, `TA` on its strand. The whole alternate is `A` there, and the clip strips it with the last base of `TA`, leaving the first base deleted: `n.1del`, one base where the record deletes two.

The deleted bases stand in a run of four `A`, 213628399 to 213628402, so the deletion can be written at three places. The one the record writes is the most 3' on the transcript's strand, where the HGVS nomenclature's 3' rule puts a change: "the most 3' position possible of the reference sequence is arbitrarily assigned to have been changed" (`hgvs-nomenclature.org/stable/recommendations/general/`). It takes the first base and the base before it; the other two lie wholly before the transcript, and none lies wholly inside.

The nomenclature's DNA deletion page says of deletions extending beyond the transcribed region: "Such deletions can only be described using genomic coordinates" (`hgvs-nomenclature.org/stable/recommendations/DNA/deletion/`). vep-rs therefore writes no HGVSc and, with no notation, neither plugin field. Under `--shift_hgvs 0` it applies no 3' rule and writes `n.1del`.
