# How a comparison treats the HGVSIntronOffset fields of an allele over a transcript's edge

This page states which values of the HGVSIntronOffset plugin's two fields a comparison of the two engines sets aside for the class `hgvs_clipped_reference_against_whole_alternate`, with three worked records. For an allele whose span runs over a transcript's edge, Ensembl VEP's plugin writes the two fields beside whatever notation its call returns, and vep-rs beside an HGVSc alone, each the intron part of its own notation's coordinate. The class, its terms (the clamp, the clip, the bases inside and outside the transcript) and its rule for the HGVSc are described under [The HGVSc of an allele whose span runs over a transcript's edge](hgvs-clipped-reference-against-whole-alternate.md). The source lines cited are of `VEP_plugins` and `ensembl-variation` at release/116.

Under `--hgvs`, Ensembl VEP 116.2 reads the whole alternate allele against the reference bases inside the transcript when an allele's span runs over the transcript's edge. vep-rs cuts the alternate allele as the clamp cut the reference and describes the change inside the transcript, or writes no HGVSc. Each engine's row carries its own notation there, or none.

The HGVSIntronOffset plugin's two fields follow that notation. The plugin writes `HGVS_IntronStartOffset` and `HGVS_IntronEndOffset` beside whatever notation `hgvs_transcript` returns (`HGVSIntronOffset.pm`, lines 62-72), and the two values are the intron parts of its coordinates (`TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 1530-1533, stored at lines 1543 and 1545). vep-rs writes them beside an HGVSc and nowhere else, each the intron part of its own notation's coordinate. The class takes the two fields in three shapes alone, and `HGVS_IntronEndOffset` alone in a fourth.

Ensembl VEP alone fills them beside a notation the class takes against an empty vep-rs one: its `ins` or `dup` of the outside bases, which describes no change of the transcript, or the `del`, `delins` or substitution of a deletion over the edge. vep-rs alone fills them where Ensembl VEP prints no notation and vep-rs an `ins` of the bases a longer allele adds inside the span, at a reverse-strand transcript's first base: 0 and 0, between two exon bases. Both fill them where both print an `ins`, in a pair of notations the class takes, and the comparator takes the field that differs.

In the second shape the class takes the two fields and leaves the row's HGVSc a difference. A reverse-strand transcript's first base lies at its genomic end, its highest coordinate. A clip that strips every base inside leaves Ensembl VEP an insertion flanked by the base past that end. That base gets no coordinate (`TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, line 2834), so Ensembl VEP writes no notation (line 1527) and its plugin neither field.

The comparator takes vep-rs's two fields there on a row of `STRAND=-1` whose cDNA position is open at its start. vep-rs's `ins` must add as many bases as the allele is longer than its Location, between two neighbouring exon coordinates. Under `n.` numbering its first coordinate is at most the position's closed end. Under `c.` numbering the place is not read.

In the fourth shape the class takes `HGVS_IntronEndOffset` alone. Ensembl VEP prints the `ins` its clip leaves, numbered from one base before a forward-strand transcript's first base, beside the pair 0 and -1. vep-rs prints the duplication that [the 3' rule](hgvs-added-bases-left-at-the-span-end.md) gives, beside the pair 0 and 0 and the count of bases it was carried in `HGVS_OFFSET`. vep-rs's end offset is the correct one: Ensembl VEP's -1 is parsed off a coordinate written for the base before the first exon, a base in no intron.

The comparator reads four conditions from the two rows. First, the row has `STRAND=1`, an allele of more bases than its Location spans, and a cDNA position open at its start and closed at its end, `?-N`, with N smaller than the Location's span. Second, Ensembl VEP's notation is an `ins` between a coordinate carrying the intron part `-1` and the same coordinate carrying none. It writes the allele's bases less the last N, beside no `HGVS_OFFSET` and the pair 0 and -1.

Third, vep-rs's notation is a `dup` of the same transcript and numbering whose coordinates carry no intron part and cover as many bases as the allele adds to its span. It stands beside the pair 0 and 0 and an `HGVS_OFFSET` of 0 or more, an absent one read as 0. Fourth, the bases from Ensembl VEP's coordinate to the duplication's last base, both counted, number N plus that `HGVS_OFFSET`. The count holds only where Ensembl VEP's coordinate is the transcript's first base: the duplication ends as many bases past the span's end, base N, as the `HGVS_OFFSET` states.

Bases are counted between exonic coordinates of one region of the transcript, so a row whose duplication lies across the start codon from Ensembl VEP's coordinate stays a difference. The shape is false on a row that carries `BAM_EDIT`: on an edited RefSeq model Ensembl VEP replaces the notation's alleles after the clip (`TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 1510-1514), so the bases its `ins` writes are not the clip's. The 1000 Genomes Phase 3 dataset on GRCh37 holds one row of this shape, the record of section 3.

The shape takes nothing else of the row. The row's HGVSc is the class only under the class's rule for the HGVSc, which does not reach a duplication carried further than the span's length plus one. The class does not take `HGVS_OFFSET`, so vep-rs's stays a difference.

An `ins` the clip leaves at an exon's first base after a true intron also prints the pair 0 and -1, and cannot meet the conditions. It writes the allele's bases less every reference base the clip was handed. The second condition makes that count N and the first makes N smaller than the span, which only the clamp at a transcript's first base does. A record that is itself an insertion prints the pair -1 and 0 there, beside a closed cDNA position.

In each shape a side's two values must be the intron parts of its own notation, read as described below, and beside vep-rs's fields neither row may carry an `HGVS_OFFSET`, except vep-rs's row in the fourth shape. Beside an empty Ensembl VEP notation, a vep-rs `del` leaves the two fields a difference, though its HGVSc pair is the class. A `dup` there, and an `ins` there of another length, under `n.` numbering away from the bases inside, on a forward-strand transcript's row or on a row open at its end, leave the fields differences, as does a closed cDNA position. Beside an Ensembl VEP `dup`, vep-rs's `ins` leaves them differences too. Any other pair of values stays a difference.

The `ins` of this class is the one `_clip_alleles` leaves of a longer reference, with its start one above its end (`TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 2245-2248). `hgvs_transcript` holds such an `ins` from its 3' flank to its 5' flank and orders the pair for printing afterwards (lines 1535-1538). It leaves the pair as held where the 5' flank carries the star of a coordinate past the stop codon (line 1537) or either flank is counted back from the stop codon's last base, a coordinate printed `*-N` (lines 2854-2860). Its start offset is the intron part of the coordinate printed second, or of the one printed first in those two cases, and lines 1530-1533 read no intron part off a `*-N` coordinate.

The `ins` of a record that is itself an insertion is held the other way round (`Utils/Sequence.pm`, lines 592-593). Where its first printed coordinate carries no star, the comparator reads an `ins` as the clip's, so such a pair of two different values stays a difference. Where it carries a star, either `ins` is held in its printed order, and the pair is read in that order unless a coordinate is printed `*-N`.

A `dup` is read in its printed order, and one printed with a single coordinate, such as `c.-49dup`, gives that coordinate's intron part as both values (line 1531). A `del`, a `delins` and a substitution are read the same way. A notation the comparator can read no pair from, such as an `ins` printed with one coordinate or any notation that holds a `*-N` coordinate, leaves the two fields a difference.

## 1. The HGVSIntronOffset fields beside the insertion of an outside base

**In short.** `21 30428125 . CCTTA TCTTA,C`, a 1000 Genomes Phase 3 record on GRCh37, has an alternate of the reference's five bases that changes the first of them alone, the `C` at 30428125. ENST00000432178, on the reverse strand, runs from 30428126 to 30433697, so 30428125 lies one base past its last base and the four bases inside are unchanged. Both engines write `3_prime_UTR_variant` and `cDNA_position` `553-?`. Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000432178.1:c.*234+1_*234insA`, `HGVS_IntronStartOffset=1` and `HGVS_IntronEndOffset=0`; vep-rs writes no HGVSc and neither field.

The notation is the one of [section 4 of the class's page](hgvs-clipped-reference-against-whole-alternate.md#4-every-base-inside-unchanged): the clip strips the four unchanged bases inside and leaves an insertion of the outside base, which `_get_cDNA_position` places at the last base with `+1`. `hgvs_transcript` holds that coordinate as the notation's start and `*234` as its end, and stores their intron parts, 1 and 0, which the plugin writes. The start offset 1 is counted at 30428125, where the transcript has no intron. No base of the transcript changes, so vep-rs writes no notation and neither of the two fields.

## 2. The HGVSIntronOffset fields beside bases added at a reverse-strand transcript's first base

**In short.** `10 25011687 . GCCGCCCCT GCCGCCCCTCCGCCCCT,G`, a 1000 Genomes Phase 3 record on GRCh37, repeats the eight reference bases at 25011688-25011695 in its first alternate. ENST00000463892, on the reverse strand, has its first base, n.1, at 25011689, so six bases of the span lie past its genomic end and two inside. Both engines write `non_coding_transcript_exon_variant`, `cDNA_position` `?-2` and `STRAND=-1`. Ensembl VEP writes no HGVSc and neither field; vep-rs writes `HGVSc=ENST00000463892.1:n.2_3insAGGGGCGG`, `HGVS_IntronStartOffset=0` and `HGVS_IntronEndOffset=0`.

Ensembl VEP's clip strips the two bases inside from the end of the sixteen alternate bases, leaving an insertion at slice span (1, 0). Position 0 is 25011690, past the last exon in genomic order, so line 2834 returns no coordinate, line 1527 no notation and `HGVSIntronOffset.pm` line 62 no field. vep-rs cuts the six outside bases from the alternate, and its clip leaves the eight added bases between n.2 and n.3, two bases of the first exon. The comparator takes both fields and leaves the HGVSc a difference: the `ins` adds eight bases, sixteen less the Location's eight, n.2 is the closed end of `?-2`, and 0 and 0 are the intron parts of n.2 and n.3.

## 3. The HGVSIntronOffset fields beside an insertion and a duplication

**In short.** `5 169931042 . TGGCAGCA TGGCAGCAGGCAGCA,T`, a 1000 Genomes Phase 3 record on GRCh37, repeats the seven reference bases at 169931043-169931049 in its first alternate. ENST00000390656, on the forward strand, starts at 169931048, c.-529, so five bases of the span lie before its first base and two inside. Both engines write `5_prime_UTR_variant` and `cDNA_position` `?-2`. Ensembl VEP writes `HGVSc=ENST00000390656.4:c.-529-1_-529insGGCAGCAGGCAG`, `HGVS_IntronStartOffset=0` and `HGVS_IntronEndOffset=-1`; vep-rs writes `HGVSc=ENST00000390656.4:c.-517_-511dup`, `HGVS_OFFSET=17`, `HGVS_IntronStartOffset=0` and `HGVS_IntronEndOffset=0`.

As in section 2, Ensembl VEP's clip leaves an insertion at slice span (1, 0), here of twelve bases. Position 0 is 169931047, before the first exon, which `_get_cDNA_position` prints as that exon's cDNA start with `-1` (lines 2802 and 2822-2826). `hgvs_transcript` stores the intron parts of `-529` and `-529-1`, 0 and -1, the end offset counted where the transcript has no intron. vep-rs's clip leaves the seven added bases between c.-528 and c.-527, and the 3' rule carries them 17 bases on, where they repeat c.-517 to c.-511, bases of the first exon: 0 and 0.

The fourth shape takes `HGVS_IntronEndOffset`. The allele holds 14 bases on a span of 7, and the cDNA position `?-2` gives N as 2. Ensembl VEP's `ins` stands at `-529-1` and `-529` and writes 12 bases, the allele's 14 less its last 2. vep-rs's `dup` covers 7 bases, the 14 less the span's 7, beside `HGVS_OFFSET=17`. From c.-529 to c.-511 are 19 bases, 2 and 17. The row's HGVSc and its `HGVS_OFFSET` stay differences wherever a comparison reads those two columns: the duplication lies 12 to 19 bases from Ensembl VEP's coordinates, and the class's rule for the HGVSc reaches 8, the span's length plus one.

The seven added bases given alone, an insertion at 5:169931049-169931050, the span's end, get vep-rs's row from both engines: `c.-517_-511dup`, `HGVS_OFFSET=17` and the pair 0 and 0. Ensembl VEP so gives one sequence two end offsets, and its -1 comes from how the record is written.
