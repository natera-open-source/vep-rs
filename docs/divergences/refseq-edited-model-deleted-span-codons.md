# The reference codons of a deletion spanning the bases a RefSeq model's alignment deleted

**The defect in one sentence.** For a deletion over bases a RefSeq model's alignment deleted, with mapped bases on both sides, Ensembl VEP reads the reference codons, their amino acids and its in-frame test from the genomic allele spliced over the shorter coding span, not from the model's bases.

**Why this is a defect.** The reference codons name the model's bases at the protein positions the row prints, and the reference amino acids are the model's residues there. The splice assumes the reference allele has the coding span's length. Where the allele is longer, the codon window reads the allele's first bases, some of which lie in the genome alone, and the model's final bases of the span fall out of it. Ensembl VEP translates that window for the reference amino acids, so a residue can be one the model's translation does not hold, and its in-frame deletion test reads the same window. The alternate codons and amino acids, read without the splice, agree on both engines.

**What vep-rs does instead.** vep-rs reads the reference codons from the model's coding sequence at the row's protein positions, as it does for every other allele. The reference amino acids are their translation, and the in-frame deletion test reads those codons. Upper case starts at the deletion's first coding base and runs for the allele's length, to the window's end at most, on both engines.

**Where it is in Ensembl VEP's source** (`ensembl-vep` at release/116.3, `ensembl-variation` at release/116):

- `Bio/EnsEMBL/Variation/TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 826-827 and 872-874, `codon`: on a model carrying an `_rna_edit` attribute, the reference allele's bases are spliced over the coding span, `substr($cds, $cds_start - 1, $vf_nt_len) = $seq`. The splice replaces `$vf_nt_len` bases with `length($seq)` bases, and line 877 then reads `$codon_len` bases from the first codon's start.
- `Bio/EnsEMBL/Variation/TranscriptVariation.pm`, lines 441-468, `_get_ref_allele`: under `--use_transcript_ref`, which a BAM-edited cache switches on (`Bio/EnsEMBL/VEP/CacheDir.pm`, lines 411-414), the model's own bases become the reference allele only when the span maps to at most two segments (line 459). A span over deleted bases maps to three, so the genomic bases stand.
- `Bio/EnsEMBL/TranscriptMapper.pm`, lines 168-221, `_load_mapper`: a deletion edit pairs no cDNA position with the deleted genomic bases (lines 213-218), which is why the span holds fewer coding positions than genomic bases.
- `Bio/EnsEMBL/Variation/TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 703 and 724-736, `peptide`: the reference allele's amino acids are the translation of that codon string.
- `Bio/EnsEMBL/Variation/Utils/VariationEffect.pm`, lines 1161-1186, `inframe_deletion`: the test reads the same codon string, through `_get_codon_alleles` (lines 812-830 of that file). It accepts where the reference codons begin or end with the alternate codons (line 1178), or where trimming the bases the two share at each end leaves none of the alternate's and whole codons of the reference's (lines 1181-1185). Where it accepts by neither, `protein_altering_variant` (lines 375-392) stands.

**What correcting it would change.** Every such row's reference codons would be the model's bases over its protein positions, the same string vep-rs writes, and its reference amino acids their translation. A row whose spliced codons fail the in-frame test would read `inframe_deletion`. The alternate codons and amino acids and the positions would not change.

**The class it produces.** `refseq_edited_model_deleted_span_codons`: three cells of a `Transcript` row carrying `BAM_EDIT=OK` whose `USED_REF` has more bases than its CDS span. The first is the Codons: the two engines' alternate codons equal and their reference codons of one length agreeing at every lower-case base and differing in an upper-case one. Ensembl VEP's upper-case bases spell the leading bases of the spliced allele on the model's strand. The second is the Amino_acids beside such Codons: the alternate halves equal, and each reference half the translation of its own engine's reference codons. The third is the Consequence beside such Codons: Ensembl VEP's `protein_altering_variant` against vep-rs's `inframe_deletion`, every other term shared, where the in-frame test refuses Ensembl VEP's codons and accepts vep-rs's.

**Which cell holds the spliced allele.** The spliced allele is the reference allele Ensembl VEP wrote over the coding span. The class reads it from `USED_REF` and, where those are not the bases the codons show, from a `GIVEN_REF` of the same length. Ensembl VEP writes the codons and the amino acids before the HGVS fields (`Bio/EnsEMBL/VEP/OutputFactory.pm`, lines 1742-1743, 1760 and 1769). Under `--hgvs`, the protein notation of a deletion that Ensembl VEP's 3' shift moves writes the shifted bases over the reference allele (`Bio/EnsEMBL/Variation/TranscriptVariationAllele.pm`, lines 1785-1786), and `USED_REF` prints them (`OutputFactory.pm`, line 1785). `GIVEN_REF` prints the allele unshifted.

Under `--refseq --hgvs` the rows of sections 1 and 2 show it. `HGVS_OFFSET` reads 1 on the rows of section 1 and 3 on the rows of section 2. `USED_REF` holds the bases of the shifted deletion and `GIVEN_REF` those of the record's own span. vep-rs prints the same `USED_REF` and `GIVEN_REF` as Ensembl VEP on those rows, and each engine's codons and amino acids are the ones it writes under `--refseq` alone.

The format comparator applies the class once per row and compares every other field. In the JSON format it leaves uncompared the `most_severe_consequence` of a record that carries the term pair. A row whose span holds a whole intron is counted under [`refseq_edited_model_intron_span_coding_reference`](refseq-edited-model-intron-span-coding-reference.md) where a comparison is given both classes.

The gnomAD v4.1 chromosome 21 dataset (GRCh38) carries 2 such rows under `--refseq` and under `--merged`, on the two COL18A1 models; the gnomAD v2.1.1 and 1000 Genomes chromosome 21 datasets carry none, and so do the committed RefSeq corpora. Genome-wide ClinVar carries 2 such rows under `--refseq` on GRCh38 and 4 on GRCh37. The amino acids differ on both GRCh38 rows and on 3 of the GRCh37 rows, and the term on 1 GRCh37 row.

## 1. A seventeen-base deletion over the nine bases COL18A1's models lack

**In short.** `NM_030582.4` and `NM_130444.3` lack the nine genomic bases `21:45504526-45504534` (edits `3387 3395` and `4092 4100`), so the seventeen bases `21:45504519-45504535`, `CAGGCCCCCCAGGCCCA`, hold eight of each model's coding positions, CDS 3371-3378 and 4076-4083. The models read `CAGGCCCA` there: `CAGGCCC` from `21:45504519-45504525`, then `A` from `21:45504535`. For the gnomAD v4.1 deletion `rs1179352929` (`21:45504518 CCAGGCCCCCCAGGCCCA C`), both engines write `frameshift_variant`, protein positions 1124-1126 and 1359-1361, amino acids `PGP/X` and the alternate codon `c`. The reference codons differ: Ensembl VEP's hold the first codon's base and the genomic allele's first eight bases, vep-rs's the models' own nine bases.

**The reference codons.** Ensembl VEP writes `cCAGGCCCC`: the first codon's base, then the genomic allele's first eight bases, ending in the `C` at `21:45504526`, a base the models lack, while the models' `A` at CDS 3378 and 4083 is not shown. vep-rs writes `cCAGGCCCA`, the models' own nine bases. On the unedited sibling `NM_001379500.1`, which has all seventeen bases, both engines write `cCAGGCCCCCCAGGCCCA/c` over protein positions 944-949. The commands of [Reproducing these examples](reproducing.md#reproducing-these-examples), with `--refseq` and the RefSeq GRCh38 cache, print these rows; `grep -E 'NM_030582.4|NM_130444.3|NM_001379500.1'` selects them.

## 2. An eighteen-base deletion over the same nine bases, three whole codons

**In short.** ClinVar's deletion `1338712` (`21:45504520 AGGCCCCCCAGGCCCACGT A`) removes `21:45504521-45504538`: five mapped bases, the nine the models lack, and four mapped bases. They are nine coding positions, CDS 3373-3381 and 4078-4086, the three codons of protein positions 1125-1127 and 1360-1362. Both engines write `inframe_deletion`, the same positions, and `-` for the alternate codons and amino acids. The reference codons and the reference amino acids differ.

**The reference cells.** Ensembl VEP writes the codons `GGCCCCCCA` and the amino acids `GPP`: the genomic allele's first nine bases, the last four of them (`21:45504526-45504529`) bases the models lack, and their translation. vep-rs writes `GGCCCACGT` and `GPR`: the models' own nine bases, `GGCCC` from `21:45504521-45504525` and `ACGT` from `21:45504535-45504538`, and the residues the models' translations hold at those positions. The second codon differs in its third base and reads proline on both engines. Ensembl VEP's third codon is `CCA`, three bases the models lack, where the models read `CGT`, arginine. The commands of [Reproducing these examples](reproducing.md#reproducing-these-examples), with `--refseq` and the RefSeq GRCh38 cache, print these rows.

## 3. A twelve-base deletion whose spliced codons fail the in-frame test

**In short.** On GRCh37 `NM_032790.4` lacks the six genomic bases `12:122064785-122064790` (`_rna_edit` `351 356`, cDNA positions). ClinVar's deletion `1415666` (`12:122064783 CCACCGCCGCCGT C`) removes `12:122064784-122064795`: one mapped base, the six the model lacks, and five mapped bases. They are six coding positions, CDS 137-142, inside the three codons of protein positions 46-48. Both engines write those positions and the alternate codons `ccc`, one proline. Ensembl VEP writes `protein_altering_variant`, the amino acids `PPP/P` and the reference codons `cCACCGCCG`. vep-rs writes `inframe_deletion`, `PPS/P` and `cCGCCGTCC`.

**The reference cells.** The model reads `CCG CCG TCC` over CDS 136-144: proline, proline, serine. Ensembl VEP's window holds the first codon's first base and the allele's first eight bases. Its third codon is two bases the model lacks and the model's CDS 138, and reads proline where the model has serine. Both engines mark `USED_REF`'s twelve bases from the deletion's first coding base, so upper case runs to the window's end, past the six bases the model loses.

**The term.** The deletion removes six of the model's coding bases and leaves `ccc`: residues 46-48 become the one proline, with nothing substituted, an in-frame deletion. Ensembl VEP's test compares `CCACCGCCG` with `CCC`. The reference codons neither begin nor end with the alternate's, and trimming the shared leading `CC` leaves `ACCGCCG` against `C`, so the test refuses and `protein_altering_variant` stands. On the model's `CCGCCGTCC` the trimming leaves `GCCGTC` and nothing of the alternate's: two whole codons, and the test accepts.

**Reproducing it.** The commands of [Reproducing these examples](reproducing.md#reproducing-these-examples), with `--refseq` and the RefSeq GRCh37 cache, print the row. On GRCh38 a deletion written for this page, `21:45504524 CCCCCAGGCCCAC C`, reaches the same test on the two COL18A1 models. Ensembl VEP writes `protein_altering_variant`, `PP/R` and `cCCCCA/cgt`; vep-rs writes `inframe_deletion`, `PR/R` and `cCACGT/cgt`. The entry's reproduction file runs it beside the records of sections 1 and 2.
