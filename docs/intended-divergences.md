# Intended divergences from Ensembl VEP

vep-rs matches Ensembl VEP exactly except for the documented divergences; this page indexes each one. Each entry leads to the record and both engines' rows.

Before you report a difference between vep-rs and Ensembl VEP, or change one, read this index and search its sections and the pages they link for the flag, field or shape. Cite an entry by its name, the text of the first cell of its row with inner backticks dropped, or by the path of its page; a change to this page's own text or order cites the page by its path. Then certify the check in the issue or the pull request, whose Intended divergences choice or section states that you touch none of the entries, names the ones you touch, or proposes a new one.

This page indexes every intended disagreement between vep-rs and Ensembl VEP 116.3: the fifteen defects and the one representation difference that produce the nineteen classes of [Concordance figures and classes](#concordance-figures-and-classes), and the differences outside those classes.

Two hundred and ninety differences lie outside every class, each in a field's text, in a header line, in a statistics report or a warning, in which records or rows are written, or in which inputs resolve. For all but nineteen nothing is set aside in the concordance figures, and a disagreement from one is charged to vep-rs in both. For `splice_term_past_the_record_span`, `pachysolen_nuclear_code_read_as_standard`, `start_codon_removed_without_five_prime_utr`, [five coding-end](concordance-provenance/116.2-divergence-classes.md#how-the-coding-ends-are-read) and [eight stop/start codon entries](concordance-provenance/116.2-divergence-classes.md#17-the-pair-classes-that-read-the-two-rows-of-a-pair) a comparison sets aside the row's differing terms and the columns that follow them; for `synonym_of_several_sequences_by_hash_order`, one given the reference's cache sets aside the reference's rows of a record vep-rs skipped.

For [`merged_same_dbid_collapse` and `merged_same_stable_id_collapse`](concordance-provenance/116.2-divergence-classes.md#18-the-classes-that-read-the-converted-cache) a comparison that names the converted cache sets aside rows of a transcript the reference dropped, and the reference's intergenic row at a record left with none.

Each entry has a page of its own, or a section of the page its subject belongs to, with the record, both engines' rows and the line of Ensembl VEP's source behind the difference.

Nineteen of these differences, and one of the defects, are reached by malformed input alone and stand in the last section of entries, [Differences only malformed input reaches](#differences-only-malformed-input-reaches).

## How the entries are ordered {#differences-outside-the-classes}

The entries are grouped by the part of a run they touch, one section each below and one after them for those only malformed input reaches, and ordered within a section from the most to the least commonly met in real-world use. The order is measured, never estimated. Every entry has a shape, the input a run must carry before the divergence can occur, and the shape is counted over two bodies of data.

An entry is one row of these tables, a mechanism is the one cause that produces its difference, and a class is the name a comparison counts its rows under. One entry stands for one mechanism: a class stated a second time, in a sentence of another section of its page, has no second entry.

The first body is five sets of chromosome 21 records, the cuts, 15,646,481 records. Three are cuts of population datasets: gnomAD v4.1 on GRCh38 (10,958,898), gnomAD v2.1.1 on GRCh37 (3,483,000) and 1000 Genomes Phase 3 on GRCh37 (1,105,538). Two are the chromosome 21 records of the ClinVar GRCh38 and GRCh37 files (49,581 and 49,464). The [1000 Genomes high-coverage chromosome 21 dataset](concordance-provenance/1kg-highcov-chr21-records-in-gnomad-v4.1-cut.json), a concordance dataset, is not among them, because the gnomAD v4.1 cut holds 998,624 of its 1,002,753 records.

The second body is the 152 golden corpora under `tests/golden/116/` with their 511 committed flag sets, a flag set being one committed run of a corpus, its flags and output formats. Beside the flag sets stand the 32 committed runs of `vep filter` and `vep recode`, the tool runs. A flag set reaches a divergence that needs a flag when it passes the flag, or a flag whose Ensembl VEP option set switches it on. `--everything` switches on its 29 flags, `--cell_type` and `--regulatory_gff` switch on `--regulatory`, `--filter_common` switches on `--check_frequency`, `--bigwig`, `--phyloP` and `--phastCons` add a `--custom` source, and `--exclude_predicted` sets a `--transcript_filter`. Every figure in a `Tier and count` cell below is that measurement.

Ordinary data is a record of the cuts, or a corpus record of a kind the cuts cannot carry: one with sample columns, or one on an organelle or the Y chromosome. It is also a row of a committed annotation table, and a transcript, regulatory feature or epigenome name of a committed cache. And it is a committed flag set or tool run whose command line is an ordinary use of a flag, plugin or option.

A line of another input format (Ensembl default, HGVS, SPDI, region or identifier), a header line, a compressed block or file, and a malformed or synthetic value in a command line are not ordinary data, whatever carries them. A row of a synthetic table is not ordinary data either: such a table holds made-up values. One that stands in the place of a plugin data file the tree does not redistribute has `synthetic` in its name; one that gives a corpus a value for every feature is listed under `synthetic_tables` in the corpus's provenance record.

Three tiers follow. Tier 1: ordinary data carries the shape and the divergence needs no flag or plugin; these entries are ordered by their rate per million records of the cuts. Tier 2: ordinary data carries the shape and the divergence needs a non-default flag, a plugin, a companion tool, or a property of a cache or table. These are ordered by the number of committed flag sets whose command line meets the divergence's requirements, then by the number of corpora whose data carries the shape, then by name.

Tier 3: ordinary data does not carry the shape; these are ordered by name. Names compare byte by byte, an underscore before a letter.

Each entry also has an input class, recorded with its basis in its reproduction. It is `observed` when real data carries its shape: at tier 1 or 2, and at tier 3 when its own page counts the shape in real data. Otherwise it is `unobserved` when the format and the documentation allow its input, and `malformed` when it breaks its format's specification or a documented contract. The last section of entries holds the malformed entries under the same categories, and the order above holds inside each section.

A rate is an upper bound: the shape is the condition a record must meet, and the transcript, the table or the cache decides whether the divergence then occurs. A shape no cut can carry is counted on the committed corpora that carry it. Such a shape is a record on an organelle or the Y chromosome, a sample column, a line of another input format, a row of an annotation table, a cache record, a flag value, a tool run, a compressed block or a whole file. Its row names what it counts, `267 of 1,198 MT records`, `1 of 511 flag sets` or `0 of 4 tool runs`, in place of a rate.

For `nearest_exon_jb_item_order` and `utrannotator_annotation_pair_order` the rate is counted from the output rows of the stated command, since their trigger, a value of two or more items, is a property of the annotation and not of the input record.

A tier 3 row counting `0 of 511 flag sets` names a value no committed run passes. A tier 3 row counting more than zero flag sets names a malformed or synthetic value a committed run passes on purpose, such as the one flag set naming a plugin written to fail. The five tool entries count the tool runs of their tool, and their rows say `tool runs` where the others say `flag sets`.

Each row states the entry's name, the difference in one sentence, its tier with the count that placed it, the Ensembl VEP release the entry was last verified against, and its page. A tier 2 or tier 3 cell adds the flag sets that reach the divergence and the corpora whose data carries its shape; a tier 1 cell says that no flag is needed. The name is the class the golden harness, the warnings comparison, the plugin tuple scorer (`tools/vep-plugin-pairs`) or the format, statistics, SNP/indel or structural-variant comparator sets the entry's rows aside under, where one does. [How to read the counts](#how-to-read-the-counts) defines all but the scorer.

Ensembl VEP corrects some of its defects from release to release, so every entry is run again on both engines whenever vep-rs moves to a newer Ensembl VEP reference. An entry the new release closes, the engines agreeing, is retired with the release that closed it, from this page, its class, its comparator mask and its manifest rows alike. An entry that changes, the engines differing in another way, is traced to its mechanism again before its row and its reproduction are rewritten.

Each entry has a reproduction under `scripts/divergences/reproductions/`: the record, the flags, the committed cache or data it reads, the field or row where the engines differ and both engines' recorded values. Its `implementation` table names the code, the tests and the comparison rules that implement the entry. `tools/vep-divergence-reevaluate` runs every reproduction against a named Ensembl VEP image and reports each entry as still present, closed or changed, or as an error when its reproduction cannot run, which is a defect in the reproduction to fix. The `Verified against` cell is the release of the last such run, and `vep-divergence-reevaluate --check` refuses a cell that names any release but the current reference.

A reader checks the order from the cells alone: down each section the tier never falls, within a tier the figure that orders it never rises, and rows equal on every figure stand in name order.

## Concordance figures and classes

vep-rs reports two concordance figures against Ensembl VEP release 116.3. **Raw concordance** keeps every tuple both engines write. A tuple is one output row reduced to its Location, Allele, Feature, Feature_type and Consequence set. Raw F1 counts the tuples the two engines share against the tuples each writes alone, with nothing set aside. **Adjusted concordance** sets aside the tuples of the divergence classes documented on these pages and nothing else.

A class is one shape of disagreement. Each class has been traced to a specific mechanism in one engine's source, reproduced with a command a reader can run, and judged. The record it is reproduced on is public, or hand-written and printed in full on the class's page.

Seventeen of the nineteen classes are the output of fourteen defects in Ensembl VEP's source that vep-rs does not reproduce. Class 5 is the records Ensembl VEP 116 drops before annotation: it writes no row for them in any output format, and vep-rs annotates them, an incomplete `<DEL>` excepted, which it drops too. With no Ensembl VEP row to judge, vep-rs's rows are set aside. Class 12, `<CNV:TR>`, is a difference of representation in which neither engine is wrong. A disagreement that is not one of these classes is charged to vep-rs in both figures, whatever its cause.

## How the pages are organised

The pages are organised by defect. There is one page for each of the fifteen defects that produce a class (A to C and E to P; D is retired) and one for the `<CNV:TR>` representation difference. Each defect page opens with the defect in one sentence, why it is a defect, what vep-rs does instead, where it is in Ensembl VEP's source and what correcting it would change. The classes it produces follow. Each of the two hundred and ninety [differences outside the classes](#differences-outside-the-classes) has a page of its own, or a section of its subject's page: the difference in one sentence, then what each engine does and where in Ensembl VEP's source its behaviour is.

Each class is a walkthrough of one record, from a public dataset or hand-written and printed in full on the page. It opens with an **In short** paragraph that states the class in a few sentences. It then gives the background a reader needs, the record, both engines' rows and why Ensembl VEP writes what it writes. It closes with why vep-rs's row is the correct one, what a reader can reproduce, and how the comparator scripts recognise the class. The engines, inputs, caches and commands every walkthrough uses are on [Reproducing these examples](divergences/reproducing.md).

## The paper's classes

The accompanying paper measured vep-rs against Ensembl VEP release 115.2, and its supplement documents five classes at the paper's pinned build, four of them defects: defects A to D below and the representation difference. Against release 115.2 these pages count those five and Defect E as twelve numbered classes, 1 to 12, and release 116 changed the behaviour behind two of the defects.

Defect C, a record marked skipped but annotated against whatever its batch had loaded, is corrected in release 116 by dropping the record before annotation. Its classes 5 to 8 compared the two engines' transcript sets on such a record. Against 116.3 there is no Ensembl VEP row to compare, so class 5 names the dropped records themselves and classes 6 to 8 are retired.

Defect D, transcripts matched to records by coordinate alone, is corrected in release 116.1 by a chromosome-aware overlap. Its classes 9 and 10 have no rows against it and are retired, and the same overlap introduces two shapes of its own, classes 23 and 24. Defects A, B and E and the `<CNV:TR>` representation are unchanged. Class numbers are identifiers and are kept as the paper's and the comparators' reports use them: 6 to 10 are retired, and 23 and 24 are placed with the mechanism that produces them.

## How to read the counts

The divergence pages share these scoring terms:

- **Tuple**: one output row reduced to its Location, Allele, Feature, Feature_type and Consequence set.
- **Raw and adjusted figure**: raw F1 counts the tuples the two engines share against the tuples each writes alone, with nothing set aside. Adjusted F1 sets aside the tuples of the classes on these pages and nothing else.
- **Set aside**: a tuple set aside is left out of the adjusted figure. Setting a tuple aside is also called **masking**: a class is fully masked when its rule sets aside every tuple of the class, and a rule is shape-limited when it sets aside only pairs of one exact shape.
- **Charged**: a disagreement that is not one of these classes is charged to vep-rs in both figures, whatever its cause.
- **Pair**: one tuple from each engine that the comparator matches as two readings of one record on one transcript. For classes 1 to 4 and 11 the two tuples share Location, Allele, Feature and Feature_type and differ in the consequence set. For class 12 they share Feature and Feature_type only, because the two engines write the same record at a different Location and Allele ([12.3](divergences/cnv-tr.md#123-what-each-engine-writes)).
- **Pooled**: counts are per input file, and the comparator also reports a pooled count per assembly in which a tuple that recurs in two files is counted once.
- **The reference** and **the candidate**: the reference is Ensembl VEP's output for the same inputs, which the comparators score vep-rs against; `--reference-release` chooses the release, and `116.3` is the default. The candidate is vep-rs's output, the side under test.
- **The comparators**: the SNP/indel comparator is `tools/vep-outputs-compare`, for the six SNP/indel datasets; the structural-variant comparator is `tools/vep-sv-compare`, for the two structural-variant sets.
- **The registry**: the 116.3 registry is the list of classes the comparators apply against Ensembl VEP 116.3, named by identifiers such as `reference_skipped_record`. It sets each class aside or counts it, as [Scoring against Ensembl VEP 116.3](#scoring-against-ensembl-vep-1163) describes.

The pages of the differences outside the classes, and the plugin pages, name four other comparisons and the terms of their configuration:

- **The format comparator**: `tools/vep-formats-compare`, which compares one Ensembl VEP output with one vep-rs output in one output format (default, tab, VCF or JSON), every field of every row verbatim. It applies the mask classes a comparison case or a corpus manifest names, counts each row it sets aside once under its class, and reports every other difference.
- **The warnings comparison**: the format comparator's `--warnings` mode, which compares the two engines' `_warnings.txt` files as sets of input line number and message, the quoted input line and any path masked.
- **The golden harness**: the test harness under `crates/vep-cli/tests/` (`common/mod.rs`), which runs vep-rs on every golden corpus under `tests/golden/116/` and compares its outputs with the Ensembl VEP 116.3 outputs committed beside each corpus, under the corpus manifest.
- **The oracle**: the comparison oracle: the harness that runs Ensembl VEP and vep-rs on the same inputs and compares every column of every row in every output format (`scripts/concordance/feature_oracle.sh`). It runs the comparison cases of a case table in two tiers: the fixture tier's cases take the golden corpora's inputs, the population tier's the measured datasets. The format comparator makes each comparison of two outputs in one output format; the statistics comparator, `tools/vep-stats-compare`, compares the two statistics reports of a case that names `stats` among its formats.
- **Comparison case** and **`masks`**: one row of the oracle's case table: an input, a cache, the flags, the output formats compared and `masks`, the mask classes the comparison applies. A mask class is a named shape of difference set aside and counted under its name; a class the case does not name is compared like any other field. A class's **predicate** is the rule that recognises its shape from the two outputs alone; a class without one applies from a corpus manifest alone.
- **Corpus manifest**, **flag set** and **row key**: a golden corpus's `manifest.json` names its records and its flag sets, each a name, the flags added to the run, the output formats compared and the directory of committed Ensembl VEP outputs. A row key is the Location, Allele, Feature and Feature_type of one output row, with the record it belongs to; the manifest's lists name rows by it, and the comparators pair the two engines' rows by it.
- **`divergences`**, **`vep_rs_only_tuples`** and **`field_divergences`**: the three lists of documented rows a manifest, and each of its flag sets, carries, each entry under its class. `divergences` names a row key whose consequence set differs, with the set vep-rs writes; `vep_rs_only_tuples` the row keys vep-rs alone writes; `field_divergences` a row key whose named field differs, with the value vep-rs prints. The golden harness and the format comparator check vep-rs's row against the documented shape in place of Ensembl VEP's, and a documented row that vep-rs writes another way is a stale divergence, reported as a difference.
- **`vep_rs_consequence_sets_with_reference_sequence`**: a key a `divergences` entry may carry: the consequence sets of the rows vep-rs writes on the row key in a run that has a reference sequence (a genome FASTA file), where the entry's `vep_rs_consequence_sets` lists those of a run without one. Where this key's one set is `vep_consequence_set`, Ensembl VEP's set, the two engines agree in a run with a reference sequence. The key is written by hand: `classify`, the golden corpus builder's subcommand that writes the lists (`tools/vep-golden-corpus`), adds none of its own and copies it onto the entry it writes again for the same Location, Allele, Feature, records and Ensembl VEP set. Where it cannot, `classify` exits with an error and leaves the manifest unchanged.
- **`--drop-reference-statements`**: the builder's `select` and a `flag-set` rewrite drop the lists `classify` writes, and refuse to discard an entry that carries `vep_rs_consequence_sets_with_reference_sequence` unless this option is given.
- **`--reference-sequence`**: the format comparator checks a row against `vep_rs_consequence_sets_with_reference_sequence` under `--reference-sequence present` alone; under `absent`, and without the option, it checks against `vep_rs_consequence_sets`, even in a run that has a reference sequence. The golden harness makes that choice itself, by whether the run's arguments and cache directory give it one.

The **six SNP/indel datasets** are ClinVar GRCh37 and GRCh38 (`clinvar_grch37.vcf.gz`, `clinvar_grch38.vcf.gz`), gnomAD v2.1.1 chr21 (GRCh37, `gnomad_genomes_v2.1.1_chr21.vcf.gz`), gnomAD v4.1 chr21 (GRCh38, `gnomad_genomes_v4.1_chr21.vcf.gz`), 1000 Genomes Phase 3 chr21 (GRCh37, `1kg_integrated_chr21.vcf.gz`) and 1000 Genomes high-coverage chr21 (GRCh38, `1kg_highcov_chr21.vcf.gz`), each scored as one file.

## Records Ensembl VEP 116.3 cannot process

Two shapes end an Ensembl VEP 116.3 run with no output for any record of the file. Nothing is set aside for them: the reference wrote no output for the run, so there is no row to compare against and no class. They are listed because vep-rs reads the same files to completion, with exit code 0 and every other record annotated.

**A coordinate at or above 2^62, 10^30 or `Inf` under `--max_sv_size -1`.** The records that reach it are:

- a `<DEL>` whose `END` is 2^62, 2^63, 2^64, 10^30 or `Inf`, or whose `SVLEN` is 2^62 - 1 or more (`SVLEN=4611686018427387903`); the 2^62 record is:

  ```
  21 30425650 x N <DEL> . . SVTYPE=DEL;END=4611686018427387904
  ```

- a `<BND>` whose `END2` is such a number;
- a bracket mate at 10^30 (`N[21:1000000000000000000000000000000[`);
- any record whose POS is 10^30 (this one in every run, the default cap included).

The forked worker dies on the coordinate and the parent aborts the run with exit code 2 and no output file:

```
ERROR: Forked process(es) died: read-through of cross-process communication detected
```

(`Runner.pm` line 583, `_forked_buffer_to_output`). Under the default `--max_sv_size` the spans are oversize and the run completes, each such record dropped with the usual warning:

```
variant size (4611686018396962253) is bigger than --max_sv_size (10000000)
```

vep-rs refuses the record whose `END` or POS is past 2^62 - 1 at warning level (`Invalid END: '4611686018427387904'`, `Invalid POS`) and annotates the rest of the file. It saturates a `SVLEN` span at 2^62 - 1, its coordinate bound. Under the default cap that record is warned as oversize and dropped, as the span is the bound's and not the record's; under `--max_sv_size -1` it is annotated against every transcript from POS to the end of the chromosome.

**A `<CNV:TR>` repeat unit of no bases under `RB`, with a FASTA.** The record is:

```
21 30425587 x N <CNV:TR> . . SVTYPE=CNV;END=30425596;SVLEN=9;RUS=,AGC;RB=3,12
```

The expansion divides each allele's `RB` by its unit's length (`Parser/VCF.pm` line 414, `shift(@RB) / length($seq)`), and an empty unit's length is 0, so Ensembl VEP dies with exit code 255 and no output file:

```
Illegal division by zero at /opt/vep/src/ensembl-vep/modules/Bio/EnsEMBL/VEP/Parser/VCF.pm line 414.
```

Without a FASTA the record is not expanded and the run completes. vep-rs reads the record symbolically with or without one, keeping the symbolic gain over the run where Ensembl VEP divides by the empty unit (`21:30425588-30425596 tandem_repeat` with `feature_elongation,coding_sequence_variant` on ENST00000390690), and completes the run.

## Scoring against Ensembl VEP 116.3

The comparators score against one Ensembl VEP release at a time, chosen by `--reference-release`, and `116.3` is the default. Against it they apply the classes of these pages, and every class the 116.3 registry sets aside or counts is listed with its treatment in [docs/concordance-provenance/116.2-divergence-classes.md](concordance-provenance/116.2-divergence-classes.md). Under `--reference-release 115.2`, the release the paper was measured against, they apply the paper's twelve classes instead, classes 1 to 12, and write exactly the reports they wrote for the paper. The five masks that carried the paper's classes 5 to 10, classes 5 and 7 sharing one, are listed as not applied in every 116.3 report of the structural-variant comparator, each with its reason.
